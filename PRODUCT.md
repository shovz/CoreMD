# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Internal medicine residents, typically in years 1-3 of residency. Using CoreMD to study Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine (21st Edition) during downtime between shifts, late-night review sessions, or focused exam prep. High cognitive load context: tired, time-pressured, already holding a lot in working memory. They need content to surface clearly and quickly.

## Product Purpose

CoreMD is a structured study platform grounded in Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine. It provides chapter and section reading, an MCQ question bank, clinical case studies, and a RAG-based AI assistant for querying Harrison's content. The measure of success is whether a resident can sit down, focus immediately, and extract what they need without fighting the interface.

## Brand Personality

Scholarly, warm, trustworthy. Like a mentor's annotated copy of Harrison's — dense with substance but never cold. The interface should feel like it was designed by someone who has read every page and knows exactly what matters.

## Anti-references

- **Duolingo / gamified education apps**: streaks, mascots, cartoonish color fills, badge walls, confetti. Condescending for postgraduate medical learners.
- **EHR / hospital software**: cramped table layouts, clinical-gray surfaces, enterprise density, modal-heavy flows, zero delight. Residents already spend enough time in Epic.

## Design Principles

1. **Content is the product.** The interface should disappear. Typography, spacing, and hierarchy exist to make Harrison's content land — not to showcase the design system.
2. **Trust through clarity.** Every label, state, and interaction must be unambiguous. Residents under cognitive load cannot afford to guess.
3. **Warmth without softness.** Approachable enough that a tired resident doesn't feel punished, but serious enough that the tool commands respect.
4. **Earn density.** Information-dense layouts are correct for this audience — but only when the density serves learning. Clutter is not density.
5. **State is sacred.** Progress, bookmarks, history, exam results — these are never decorative. Treat them as clinical data.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Specific priorities: color must never be the only indicator of meaning (difficulty levels, correctness, etc.), all interactive elements must have visible focus states, keyboard navigation must be complete, reduced-motion preference must be respected.
